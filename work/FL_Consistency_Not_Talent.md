# Consistency, Not Talent (and Frame, Not Upstage)

## 1. Intentional Design Choices (Simple Visual Identity)
To make my portfolio feel intentional rather than amateur, I adopted a minimal and consistent visual identity. I chose a sleek, low-contrast geometric pattern for my hero texture using my brand colors (#4F46E5 on #FAFAFA). This provides a highly technical, rigorous feel without overwhelming the senses. 

## 2. Framing, Not Upstaging
The golden rule of a portfolio is that the design must frame the work, never upstage it. I maintained this by ensuring the typography and color palette remain strictly in the background. My research and data are the stars of the show; the design exists only to make that data legible and trustworthy.

## 3. Judging AI Images & Embracing Reality
While AI can generate hundreds of images in minutes, the real skill is discernment.

**The Rejection:** 
I deliberately rejected an AI-generated image of a glowing "cyberpunk AI brain" (`rejected_ai_image.png`). It was noisy, distracting, and violated my core style note. A senior engineer's portfolio should look like a rigorous research document, not a sci-fi movie poster.

**Knowing When Reality Wins:**
I actively chose *against* using AI-generated avatars or "dashboard graphics." 
- For my "About Me" section, I used a real photo because an AI avatar immediately breaks trust with technical recruiters.
- For my "Content Opportunity Scoring Model" case study, I used a raw, real screenshot of my DuckDB output from my Colab notebook. Real data proves that I actually did the work, and a real screenshot will always beat a polished but fake generated graphic.
