Predicting Content Decay at Scale: An Honest Machine Learning Approach

By Assem ElQersh

Abstract

Identifying which search queries are silently losing traffic is a critical challenge for high-volume content platforms. This research leverages a massive, real-world data warehouse snapshot (v20260703) containing over 81.7 million rows of production search data to predict content decay using historical performance metrics. Working with an unbalanced panel star schema, we engineered an honest validation pipeline that strictly isolates client data (Grouped Split) and eliminates time-window target leakage. While a naive baseline model reported a 98% precision score due to feature leakage, our rigorous methodology produced a realistic 36% Precision@50, proving that standard cross-validation heavily overestimates true predictive power in temporal search data. This model serves as the foundation for our ranked editorial action playbook, prioritizing high-impact content refreshes proactively.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

The FlyRank platform manages millions of URLs. Content naturally decays over time as competitors update their pages, search algorithms shift, or search intent changes. Waiting for traffic to drop to zero before taking action results in severe, irrecoverable lost revenue.

The Objective: Can we predict which pages are beginning to decay (lose impression share) based on historical search metrics, content age, and past performance, so that the editorial team can refresh them before they lose their rankings?

This research supports proactive, automated decision-making by surfacing a prioritized, actionable queue of "at-risk" URLs for the editorial team without relying on flawed, time-traveling predictive models.

Data Architecture & Complexity

  • Unbalanced Panel Data: The dataset exhibits severe class and structural imbalances. As defined in the `dim_clients` table, per-client history depth differs significantly based on when a client onboarded (`gsc_data_start` and `gad_data_start`), requiring time-aware handling.
  • Privacy & Security: All records use salted, namespaced, and fingerprinted hash keys (e.g., `client_hash_id`). Exact search queries, PII, and client names were rigorously excluded to ensure public safety.
  • Feature Space: Predictive features utilized include days_since_last_update, word_count, avg_position, and content_age_days, aggregated over distinct historical windows.

3. Methodology

To build an honest predictive model, we had to dismantle several structural flaws that plague standard machine learning setups when dealing with panel data:

  1. The Label Definition: We defined "decaying" (is_declining = 1) as content where the calculated trend_direction == 'down' based on recent vs. historical baselines.
  2. The Leakage Audit (No Time Machines): The trend_direction is mathematically derived from impressions_last_30d and impressions_prev_30d. Including impressions_last_30d or impressions_90d as predictive features gave the model explicit knowledge of the future (Target Leakage). We strictly audited and dropped these leaky features.
  3. Honest Validation Design (Grouped Split): A naive random split allows pages from the same client to exist in both the training and testing sets. Because clients have distinct traffic baselines and onboarding dates, the model could "cheat" by memorizing client profiles. We utilized a GroupShuffleSplit on client_id to ensure the model was evaluated on completely unseen clients.

4. Results (vs baseline)

The performance delta between the flawed baseline models and our honest model exposed massive score inflation commonly found in industry benchmarks:

Methodology Precision@50
Naive Random Split (Leaky Features) 98.0% (Inflated)
Honest Grouped Split (Leaky Features) 58.0%
Honest Grouped Split (No Leakage) 36.0% (True Capability)

By forcing the model to generalize to new clients and removing its "time machine," we established a realistic baseline. The final 36.0% precision represents the true, honest capability of the model in production when facing unseen client data.

5. Limitations

This research operates within the following boundaries and limitations:

  • Seasonality Blindness: The model does not understand temporal intent. It will mistakenly flag seasonal pages (e.g., "Summer Clothing") as decaying when their season ends, as it cannot differentiate between cyclical drops and structural decay.
  • Evergreen Stability: Highly static pages (e.g., dictionary definitions) may be flagged due to age and low volatility. These require human review to prevent unnecessary rewrites.
  • Causal vs. Descriptive: The feature importances discovered in this analysis are strictly descriptive decision-support mechanisms, not causal drivers. Artificially modifying a page's word count will not automatically reverse its decay.

6. Ranked Recommendations (The Action Playbook)

Based on our verified methodology, we established the following ranked triage system for the editorial team. This pipeline yielded 3,400 actionable items in our final test queue:

Priority 1: The Full Refresh

Pages ranking 11-20 with high impressions (>1,000) that haven't been updated in 180+ days.
Action: Update facts, expand thin sections, and improve internal linking structure.

Priority 2: Snippet Optimization

Pages ranking on Page 1 (positions 1-10) with massive impressions but CTR < 1%.
Action: Rewrite title tags and meta descriptions to improve SERP click-through rates.

Priority 3: Content Consolidation

Pages exhibiting severe decay with overlapping semantic intent to stronger pages.
Action: 301 redirect the weaker, decaying page to the stronger authoritative page.

No-Go List: Automation scripts are strictly forbidden from automatically deleting content or rewriting legal/compliance policies based on model scores.
